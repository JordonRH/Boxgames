# Just play

## Which screen am I on?

**Shared display:** create the room on a TV/PC and leave it as a host. The shared display shows the scene, progress and scores. Players use their own screens.

**Phone host:** after creating the room, enter your name and tap **Play on this device**. You now get your own game buttons, as well as host controls. Private cards will appear here, so use a different device if you need a shared TV display.

**Player:** enter the room code or scan the QR, choose a name, and wait for the host to let you in. Stay in that tab. The game picker and short rules tell you what to do next.

## Blank Slate

Read the sentence with the blank. **Tap one card** from your five-card hand. That is your submission; no second button is needed. Your hand refills automatically.

Before you submit on a non-judging turn, you may use **Refresh all 5 cards** once per game. It replaces the entire hand with fresh cards; it cannot be used after submitting or reused after reconnecting.

The named judge does not play a card that round. Once the answers are revealed, the judge **taps their favourite answer**. Its author gets one point. The judge changes each round; everyone gets a judging turn. Missing submissions abstain. A timeout with no cards or no judge choice awards no point.

Example original prompt: “The village council spent its entire budget on ____.” One possible response: “a raccoon with a clipboard”.

## Clue Crew

The named guesser taps **I’m ready — start guessing**. The timer starts then. Their screen shows only the current category: Animals, Food & drink, Everyday objects, Actions, People & jobs, or Sports & activities. The server never sends them the live word. There is no need to turn the phone around and no word-reveal countdown.

Everyone else reads the word on their own phone or PC and describes it without saying the word, any part of it, or a rhyme. Clue-givers tap **Got it** when the guesser says it correctly. Anyone, including the guesser, can tap **Pass**. The clue reminder stays visible during play. Large buttons work without motion permissions or tilt. Simultaneous taps cannot count a word twice.

Each correct word earns the guesser one point when their turn ends. The usual turn is 60 seconds; relaxed pace is 90 seconds; untimed play can be finished by the host. Each person gets one turn. The TV hides the live word so the guesser cannot read it there.

## Crowd Pick

With **at least three players**, read the three projects and **tap one favourite**. That is your entire move. Wait for the room to choose. The most popular project wins; everyone who chose it gets one point. Tied projects share the win. Five rounds.

There are no crate budgets, rank forecasts, or second voting stage in this simpler game.

## Blackout Run

**Goal: finish with the most points after six rounds.** Tap one of four answers to a trivia question, then solve a multi-step repair puzzle. A game covers six different subjects: Space, Science, History, Books, Nature and Numbers. Choose **Casual**, **Mixed** or **Hard** in the lobby. Mixed uses three medium questions followed by three hard ones. Casual and Hard use that level for all six questions. All modes keep repair puzzles and the same scoring rules. A normal round allows 20 seconds for trivia and 15 for repair; relaxed pace doubles these, and untimed play waits for everyone. The host can pause or end; a missing answer abstains.

**Charge is a battery bonus, not an elimination counter.** Each unit left after a full six-round game adds one point to your score. Each correct trivia answer earns 2 points and restores 1 charge, up to 3. Each correct repair earns 1 point. If you miss both, lose 1 charge, down to zero. Everyone starts with 3 charge and keeps playing at zero in backup mode. A later correct trivia answer restores charge.

Points and charge bank only when both stages finish. The shared reveal shows both answers, explanation, individual points and remaining charge. Six rounds, without repeated trivia subjects or repair puzzles within a game. After round six the host taps **See final scores** to award each player's remaining charge as a bonus of 0–3 points. Ending early keeps completed-round points and grants no charge bonus. Highest total wins; equal scores tie.

## False Front

An unusual word appears. Eight example placeholders offer starting ideas; **Different example** cycles the hint without changing anything you have typed. These are writing suggestions, not real definitions. Write a believable **made-up meaning** in 3–80 characters and tap **Lock in my bluff**. If you know the word, invent something different. Exact matches to the stored definition and selected known aliases are rejected; this is not a semantic detector of every possible real definition.

Next, everyone privately sees **two meanings**: the real one and another player's bluff. Tap the real one for 2 points. Each time someone picks your bluff, you earn 1 point. Identical bluffs merge and all their authors share each fooled-voter point; none of those authors face that bluff themselves. Opponents are assigned to balance exposure, although equal exposure is not guaranteed. If no eligible bluff exists, a house bluff fills the second slot and earns nobody points.

Normal pace is 45 seconds to write and 20 seconds to vote; relaxed doubles these. Untimed play waits for everyone. Missing writers can still vote; missing voters abstain. Scores bank after voting finishes. Five rounds; tied final totals share the win. The 24-word pack stays fresh between games in this room until the host resets used content.

The host can **Skip round · no points** during writing or voting. This discards that round's text/votes and reveals only the real definition, useful if typed content needs moderation. Text otherwise stays private until it appears in a player's duel, and authors/voters stay hidden until reveal. Ending a game discards unfinished input. Boxgame does not retain a permanent text history; live round text is cleared on the next round or game end.

## If buttons are unavailable

- The **Shared display** label means that device is hosting rather than playing. Join yourself with **Play on this device** before starting.
- **Waiting for the host** means the host needs to start or advance the round.
- **You are the judge** means you wait for the others’ cards, then pick a winner.
- **Waiting for the guesser** means the named person needs to tap **I’m ready**.
- A paused/disconnected message freezes actions and explains who the room is waiting for. Reconnect in the same tab to restore your seat.

## Clue Crew: two teams

The host selects **Two teams** in the lobby, assigns players and chooses a target. There must be at least two players on each team. Team Lime goes first, then Team Coral; the guesser rotates through each team's roster. Only active teammates see the word and control Got it/Pass. The guesser sees the category and can Pass; opponents see neither the word nor a usable word identifier.

Each completed turn adds correct guesses to that team's score and to the named guesser's individual score. Reaching the target does not cut off the other team's turn. After equal turns, a team at or above the target wins if it leads; a tie continues. At six turns per team the highest score wins, with a shared tie if equal. Early ending keeps completed turns only. Fewer than two connected players on either team pauses play. Removing someone so a team has fewer than two members ends the game with a message and keeps completed-turn points.

## Rematches, session totals and fresh packs

**Play again · same settings** retains the room code, approved players and options, draws fresh content and opens the short instructions. **Choose another game** keeps everyone in the lobby. Enable **Keep a session scoreboard across games** in the lobby before playing to carry raw points from ended games. It is a casual running total; different games have different scoring scales, and team points are represented by individual guessers' earned points. Each game still starts its own score at zero. Hosts can reset totals in the lobby.

The room remembers dealt cards, used clue words and questions/prompts/terms/project sets reserved for a game. Unplayed reserved rounds are also excluded on rematch. If a pack is exhausted, the host must explicitly **Reset used content · allow repeats** in the lobby. This does not clear scores. A new room has its own fresh history. None of this survives a server restart or room expiry.

During a trivia reveal, players can flag a question as too easy, too hard, unclear or apparently incorrect. Each player gets one flag per round; only anonymous aggregate counts are shown, and scoring is unaffected. The deduplication record is transient to the game. These flags help the host discuss a question; they are not a permanent developer-feedback inbox.
